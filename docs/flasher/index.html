Stay Open
Flashing a Hammer BC01

Open firmware for the Hammer BC01. Measured on hardware: 1.6–1.7 TH/s at 750 MHz, 24 W, around 54 °C, no hardware errors.

Check your device before you flash

Retail BC01 units ship with Secure Boot enforced and both spare key slots revoked. On one of those this firmware will not boot and cannot be recovered — eFuses are permanent and USB access does not help. Ask the device first:

esptool.py --port COM3 get_security_info

If secure_boot_en is set, stop. The ESP32-S3 is a socketed LilyGO T-Display-S3, so fitting a fresh module runs this firmware with no exploit and leaves your original untouched — see SECURE-BOOT.md and HARDWARE-SWAP.md.

Open firmware for the Hammer BC04, four BM1370s in series. Measured on hardware: 6.1 TH/s at 750 MHz, 99 W, 65 °C, no hardware errors — or 5.1 TH/s at 72 W if you would rather have the efficiency.

Ethernet and WiFi both work

Ethernet on a BC04 had to be started after the hashboard is powered rather than before it — a controller already running when the core rail steps up stops answering about 70 ms later and never recovers. The firmware now starts it at the right point, and a BC04 hashing at 750 MHz serves its own interface over Ethernet with no errors.

Confirmed both ways: with WiFi alongside, and with WiFi switched off entirely — which is what this image is until you set it up. On Ethernet alone it mined at 6.2 TH/s with no rejected shares and no hardware errors.

So plug in a cable and it works. If you would rather set up over WiFi, the miner also raises an access point named HAMMER_xxxx on first boot.

KNOWN-ISSUES.md has the measurements, including the four wrong theories that came first.

We have not measured a retail BC04's eFuses

The BC01 ships with Secure Boot enforced, and this firmware cannot boot on a locked module. Whether a retail BC04 is locked the same way is unknown to us — the BC04 tested here ran on a replacement LilyGO T-Display-S3, so its original module was never read. Do not assume it is open. Ask the device:

esptool.py --port COM3 get_security_info

If secure_boot_en is set, stop and read SECURE-BOOT.md. The module is socketed on this board too.

Not for BC02, BC06 or BC08

The board model is fixed when the firmware is compiled, and the boards do not share a pinout. The BC01 and BC04 have their ASIC UART lines swapped — TX 18/RX 17 against TX 17/RX 18 — so a BC01 image on a BC04 crosses transmit and receive. Pick the wrong one above and the miner boots, the web UI works, the chip is even detected, and it never returns a single share. The firmware catches that case now and says so on screen rather than looking like broken hardware, but the flash is still wasted.

The other models are supported in the source and can be built from it; only the BC01 and BC04 are published here, because only those two have been run on real hardware.

Before you flash

This is unofficial firmware, written by an independent open-source miner. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or supported by Hammer, and flashing it may void your warranty with them.

It comes with no warranty of any kind — see sections 15 and 16 of the GPL-3.0 licence. You flash at your own risk. We are not responsible for damage caused by running a device beyond its limits, or by the flashing process leaving it unusable.

Flashing erases your configuration: WiFi credentials, pool settings and payout address all go. Note them down first.

Two protections in this firmware are known not to work, and both are documented rather than hidden: HARDWARE-SAFETY.md lists what stands between a fault and a damaged board, what has actually been tested, and what has not. Read it before you leave a miner unattended.

This browser cannot talk to serial devices. Use Chrome or Edge on a desktop, or flash with esptool instead. Serial access needs a secure context — open this page over HTTPS.

Chrome or Edge on desktop. Connect the miner's ESP32-S3 by USB‑C first.

Nothing here mines to us

The image carries no credentials. Its settings partition is built from the public template, so a freshly flashed miner starts its own WiFi access point and waits for you to enter your network, your pool and your own payout address. Set an API password while you are there — leaving it blank disables authentication, which is how the vendor firmware shipped.

Or do it yourself

Build from source ESP-IDF 5.5.1. Full source, GPL-3.0.
Flash with esptool esptool.py --chip esp32s3 write_flash 0x0 stay-open-bc01-full.bin
Update over the air Upload the -ota.bin through the miner's own update page. Keeps your settings.
