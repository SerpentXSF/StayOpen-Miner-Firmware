## The chart is the miner's history now, not the browser's

It used to be drawn from arrays the open page accumulated: close the tab and
it started empty, open it on a different device and it started empty again,
and a miner that ran all week with nobody watching had nothing to show for
the week. What it plotted was how long that tab had been open.

The firmware keeps its own record: hashrate, hashboard temperature, regulator
temperature and power, one sample every thirty seconds, held for a day.
**1H / 4H / 24H** buttons sit next to RESET on the chart. Ranges longer than
the miner has been up return what it has.

**Since boot, by design.** The record lives in RAM and is lost on reboot.
That keeps a periodic write off the flash that also holds your firmware, and
a reboot was already the end of the old chart anyway.

What it costs: 23 KB of PSRAM (2880 records of eight bytes), one extra sensor
read every thirty seconds — less bus traffic than a single open browser
already causes at its ten-second poll — and nothing on flash. Sampling rides
a loop the system task already runs rather than adding a timer.

A day at half-minute resolution is more points than a chart can draw, so the
device averages into buckets sized to what the browser asks for: 1H arrives
at full thirty-second resolution, 4H at ninety seconds, 24H at eight minutes.
A gap comes back as a gap rather than as a dive to zero that never happened.

For anyone building against it: `GET /api/system/history?window=<seconds>&points=<n>`.
It returns no timestamps on purpose — the miner's clock depends on NTP having
worked, so the response says how long ago its newest sample was taken and
lets the caller place the series against its own clock.

## Still open

**The fan-fault protection cannot fire.** The flag that would trigger it is
never set, so a stalled or failed fan will not by itself shut the miner down.
The over-temperature trip and the unreadable-sensor trip are the two thermal
protections known to work.

**A BC04 cannot de-energise its own hashboard once the I²C bus is gone.**
A miner showing an empty bus scan, a board temperature of −60 °C, or a reboot
every four minutes should have its power removed by hand.

## Testing

Installed over the air onto a BC01 and checked on the running miner: the new
endpoint, the chart drawing a device history into a browser whose own stored
series had been wiped first, and each range button. No BC04 has run this
firmware — the BC04 images are built from the same source and are untested on
hardware.

## Files

`-full.bin` is the whole flash and is what the web flasher writes over USB.
`-ota.bin` and `-www-ota.bin` are for updating in place. `SHA256SUMS` covers
every file here; check it before you flash something you downloaded.
