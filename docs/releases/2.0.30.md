## A pool offering difficulty 8192 left every job at difficulty zero

The difficulty was stored only inside a test against another copy of itself,
and the two did not start life agreeing — one began at 8192, the other at
zero. On a pool whose first `mining.set_difficulty` was exactly 8192 the
assignment was skipped, every job was built at difficulty zero, every nonce
the chip found cleared that bar and was submitted, and the pool refused very
nearly all of them.

8192 is an ordinary pool default. If your miner has ever submitted far more
shares than it should and had them refused, this is the reason to update.

## Also in this release

**The Bitcoin price no longer reads `$--` for a quarter of an hour after every
boot.** The first request went out about thirty seconds into start-up, while
Wi-Fi, the stratum sessions, the display and the ASIC ramp were all still
coming up. TLS needs internal memory that is scarce at that moment, so the
handshake completed and the read then failed — and a failed attempt was
punished with the full fifteen-minute interval. It now waits for start-up to
finish, and retries in a minute if a pass returns nothing.

**A dropped firmware upload is retried instead of handed back.** Large uploads
to the update endpoints sometimes stop mid-transfer. Nothing is damaged — a
partial write fails its checksum and is rejected, and mining carries on — and
the remedy has always been to upload it again, which the page now does by
itself, three times. A file the miner actually refuses is not retried.

**SoloLuck joins the recommended pool list**, contributed by the pool's own
operator, who disclosed as much when offering it.

**A new screen on the display**, reached with the NEXT button after the clock:
the RPM Bathwater jar, with Bitcoin facts that change on their own. It appears
on every board this firmware supports.

**Five reset reasons that reported as "Not specified"** now have names,
including USB, power glitch and CPU lockup.

**The pin bridge test no longer reports stuck-low pins as shorts.** A pin
already held low reads low whichever line is driven, so three stuck pins were
being reported as 28 bridged pairs — in output that goes into fault evidence.

## Still open

**The upload retry has not been seen to fire on a real dropped upload**,
because the failure is intermittent and could not be reproduced on demand.
The logic is straightforward and a refusal is deliberately not retried, but it
is untested in the field and worth saying so.

**A BC04 still cannot de-energise its own hashboard once I²C is gone.** There
is no second lever to give it. A miner showing an empty bus scan, a board
temperature of −60 °C, or a reboot every four minutes should have its power
removed by hand.

Known issues, with what is fixed, what is not, and which release each fix is
in, are in `docs/KNOWN-ISSUES.md`.

## Testing

**Soaked 10 hours on a BC04.** 0 reboots, 0 unreachable samples, 0 hardware
errors, 10,502 shares accepted and none rejected, and free heap flat at +29
bytes per hour across 1,198 samples. Frequency and core voltage never moved
from 640 MHz / 4.60 V.

That is shorter than the 26 hours behind 2.0.28 and 2.0.29, and it is said
here rather than left for anyone to assume otherwise. Most of what changed is
interface and diagnostics; the one change in the mining path is the difficulty
fix, which ten hours of accepted shares exercises thoroughly.

## Files

`-full.bin` is the whole flash and is what the web flasher writes over USB.
`-ota.bin` and `-www-ota.bin` are for updating in place. `SHA256SUMS` covers
every file here; check it before you flash something you downloaded.
