## Four ways this was throwing your shares away

Every one of these is in 2.0.30 and every one costs work that was already done.
If you mine to more than one pool, this is the release to take.

**Pool A kept building from a session that had already ended.** The notify it
last received was held and never released when the connection dropped, and the
guard meant to skip its turn only ever fired before pool A's *first* notify
since boot. So a pool A that worked and then dropped kept being given its share
of the slices and kept building work from a dead job id — counted as served,
submitted, refused. At the default split that is **half the miner's output**
going nowhere while the counters say otherwise.

**A secondary pool inherited the dead session's work.** Its job ids, its
extranonce and its held notify all outlived the session that issued them, so
the first shares after every reconnect were built against a job the pool had
never heard of and came back rejected — which reads as the pool being
difficult rather than as us sending it nonsense.

**Shares went to the wrong pool.** Work built for pool C or D was submitted to
pool B, which refuses it as an unknown job. Only pool B's own shares landed
where they belonged.

**A failover pool's shares were submitted under the primary's worker name.**
If your backup pool uses a different worker, the shares it accepted were
credited to the wrong one.

Also fixed: a pool A outage used to stall the other pools entirely rather than
letting them carry on, and a queue clear could park the very task that drains
it.

## Up to four pools

Two pools became four. **Three and four need a board with four ASICs** — in
practice a BC04 — and the buttons for them are disabled otherwise.

The pool page is now **Pool → Other Pools**, with a Single / Dual / Triple /
Quad selector and one identical panel per pool: host, user, password, TLS, and
its own failover stepped independently of pool A's.

**Pool A's share is the Hashrate split slider and nothing else changes it.** It
defaults to 50, so a four-pool miner left at the defaults gives pool A half and
splits the rest three ways — about 16% each, not 25. The per-pool **Share**
weights divide only what the slider leaves over. Set the slider to 25 as well
for an even four-way split.

The dashboard shows every configured pool, each with its own connection badge
and share counts, and a split bar with a segment per pool. Pool A has a
connection badge for the first time, and `poolAConnected` joins
`pool<X>Connected` in `/api/system/info`.

## Smaller things you may notice

- **A task that fails to start now says so**, and the miner reports a hardware
  fault instead of reporting itself healthy while mining nothing.
- **The log viewer no longer appears to freeze** when lines arrive faster than
  it drew them.
- **Rejection reasons are shown.** The pool was sending them all along.
- **`POST /api/system/pool/drop`** ends a secondary pool's session on demand,
  so a reconnect can be tested without unplugging anything.
- **Internal RAM is reported** — `freeHeapInternal`, `minFreeHeapInternal`,
  `largestBlockInternal` and now the DMA equivalents. On this hardware the
  total free heap is almost all PSRAM and tells you nothing about the pool that
  actually runs out.
- The pool settings page no longer describes two pools, a split it does not
  have, or a failover belonging to pool B on pool C's panel.

## Still open

**On a BC04, an HTTPS read fails roughly every thirty minutes, for as long
as the miner is up.** The certificate validates, then `esp-aes: Failed to
allocate memory` kills the read. Every earlier release described this as a
start-up problem that cleared; the 26-hour soak for this release shows it does
not clear. It fired 64 times on a BC04 over the soak, first at 4.6 hours of uptime and
last at 29.8, a median of 30 minutes apart. The request it kills is picked up by a later
endpoint in the same pass nearly every time, so an owner mostly sees nothing
beyond an occasional stale price, but the condition is permanent and not a
transient.

It is not new in this release and it is not fixed here. The AES path needs a
contiguous 1,600-byte DMA-capable buffer, and on this build a BC04's largest
contiguous DMA block averaged 1,364 bytes over 1,543 samples and was under
1,600 in 1,536 of them, 99.5%. Raising `SPIRAM_MALLOC_RESERVE_INTERNAL` to the
IDF default was tried and did not help.

> **Corrected 2026-10-19.** This section originally went on to say the
> diagnosis was settled: that a BC04 is below the threshold almost all the
> time, and that the rare moments its block clears 1,600 are why an HTTPS
> request ever succeeds. **The next soak refuted that.** A 26-hour run on the
> same board with a much roomier pool — the block above 1,600 in **97.7%** of
> samples rather than 0.5% — showed the failure rate essentially unchanged,
> 1.85 against 2.06 failures per hour. A 195-fold change in how often the
> threshold is cleared bought a 10% change in failures, which is noise.
>
> The measurements above are unchanged and still correct. What was wrong was
> treating them as the cause: 2.0.31's block sat below the threshold 99.5% of
> the time and its requests failed, and that looked like cause when it was
> coincidence. The 1,600-byte requirement is real and read from the IDF source;
> what does not follow is that the steady-state block predicts any individual
> request. Two untested candidates remain — the call needs an input *and* an
> output buffer, so two aligned blocks rather than one, and a once-a-minute
> sample is not the pool's state inside a TLS read. See
> `docs/KNOWN-ISSUES.md`. Nothing about the firmware changed; only this
> explanation did.

**A BC01 does not show this at all.** Its largest contiguous DMA block held a
steady 16,384 bytes and never fell below 1,600 in 1,542 samples, and not one
`esp-aes` line appeared on it over the whole soak. That contrast is real and
unexplained by anything else; what the correction above establishes is that it
is not enough on its own to pin the cause on the block size, since a BC04 given
a far bigger block went on failing at the same rate.

**A BC04's DMA-capable memory reaches tens of bytes during start-up**, and
stays low afterwards. Two different measurements, kept apart here because the
pools are different: across ten reboots the *internal* floor
(`minFreeHeapInternal`) ranged 131 to 1,639 bytes, and on one instrumented boot
the *DMA* floor (`minFreeHeapDma`) reached 19. Internal counts the 8 KB RTC
fast region, which carries no DMA capability, so the two are not
interchangeable. Over the soak it was far
steadier than that implies: a BC04's DMA floor moved 48 bytes in 26 hours and ended at 311, while a
BC01's ended at 2,283. There is no headroom left to trim for on
a BC04, and trimming stacks does not address it — 4,608 bytes of task stack
were returned during this release and the floor did not move.

**A down pool's slices are not shared out among the pools still up.** Work a
secondary cannot take falls through to pool A, not to the other secondaries.
Nothing is lost; the hashrate just lands somewhere other than where the weights
asked.

**Pool A's rejects are ordinary, and why they land only on pool A is not.** Over the soak a BC01 rejected 10 shares against 24,297 accepted on pool A, and a BC04
19 against 22,806 — 0.041% and 0.083% — every one of them on pool A, which
is a pool running on the local network. They have never
been traced. No secondary pool rejected a single share on either board over the
whole run.

All of these, with what is known and what is not, are in
`docs/KNOWN-ISSUES.md`.

## Testing

**26 hours on both boards, on the exact binaries this release ships.** The
app images were rebuilt from the release commit and hashed identically to
what was flashed, so both miners ran the candidate and not something near
it. **Neither board rebooted** and neither logged a hardware error.
Frequency and core voltage finished where they started, 750 MHz / 120 on the
BC01 and 640 / 460 on the BC04. A BC01 took 26,291 accepted shares and a
BC04 32,697, from 3,085 samples a minute apart. One sample out of those 3,085
failed to reach the BC01 and the next one succeeded — a network blip that
recovered itself, said here rather than rounded away.

**Every rejected share on both miners belonged to pool A** — 10 of 10 on the
BC01 and 19 of 19 on the BC04. Pools B, C and D rejected nothing at all, on
either board, across the whole run. That is the result this release was
looking for, since every fix in it concerns secondary pools being given work
from sessions that had already ended; pool A's own small reject count is an
older and separate matter: the pool reports those as `Above target` and
`Stale`, both ordinary stratum outcomes, and what is unexplained is only why
they land on pool A and not the secondaries. Recorded in
`docs/KNOWN-ISSUES.md`.

Free internal heap drifted -7 bytes/hour on the BC01 and -23 on the BC04.
Those are **not** comparable to the -25 of 2.0.29 or the +29 of 2.0.30: those
were total `freeHeap`, which on this hardware is almost all PSRAM, and
`freeHeapInternal` did not exist until after 2.0.30. This is the first release
with a figure for the pool that actually runs out, and there is nothing to
compare it against yet.

**What was verified on hardware rather than reasoned about.** The pool A fix
was measured through a Stratum relay standing in for the pool, with it
unreachable for 75 seconds: `poolAJobsServed` read the same at the moment the
session closed and at the moment the replacement session sent its first notify,
so nothing was built in between, and 41 submits came back with none rejected.
The secondary-pool fixes were measured the same way over eight forced
reconnects across all three secondary pools — nothing built before a fresh
extranonce existed, a distinct extranonce every time, nothing rejected, and the
other pools undisturbed.

The four-pool split was measured on a BC04 with the slider at 25 and each Share
at 25: within 1σ of 25% each over 2,754 shares with none rejected across two
hours.

The dashboard was confirmed on the rendered page on a BC04 running this
release: four pool blocks each with their own connection badge, a four-segment
split bar at 25% each, and per-pool share counts. Both miners were updated to
this image over the air and came back mining, past the 60-second rollback
window, before this was written.

Stack sizes were measured on both boards rather than guessed — the high-water
mark of every task read three times per board, and the sizes set from the worst
reading seen.

**What was not.** The browser flasher's Update mode has still never been run on
hardware in either direction; see `docs/KNOWN-ISSUES.md`. The upload retry has
still not been seen to fire on a real dropped upload.

## Files

`-full.bin` is the whole flash and is what the web flasher writes over USB.
`-ota.bin` and `-www-ota.bin` are for updating in place. `SHA256SUMS` covers
every file here; check it before you flash something you downloaded.
