## Four ways this was throwing your shares away

Every one of these is in 2.0.30 and every one costs work that was already done.
If you mine to more than one pool, this is the release to take.

**Pool A kept building from a session that had already ended.** The notify it
last received was held and never released when the connection dropped, and the
guard meant to skip its turn only ever fired before pool A's *first* notify
since boot. So a pool A that worked and then dropped kept being given its share
of the slices and kept building work from a dead job id — counted as served,
submitted, refused. At the default split that is **half the miner's output**
going nowhere while the counters say otherwise.

**A secondary pool inherited the dead session's work.** Its job ids, its
extranonce and its held notify all outlived the session that issued them, so
the first shares after every reconnect were built against a job the pool had
never heard of and came back rejected — which reads as the pool being
difficult rather than as us sending it nonsense.

**Shares went to the wrong pool.** Work built for pool C or D was submitted to
pool B, which refuses it as an unknown job. Only pool B's own shares landed
where they belonged.

**A failover pool's shares were submitted under the primary's worker name.**
If your backup pool uses a different worker, the shares it accepted were
credited to the wrong one.

Also fixed: a pool A outage used to stall the other pools entirely rather than
letting them carry on, and a queue clear could park the very task that drains
it.

## Up to four pools

Two pools became four. **Three and four need a board with four ASICs** — in
practice a BC04 — and the buttons for them are disabled otherwise.

The pool page is now **Pool → Other Pools**, with a Single / Dual / Triple /
Quad selector and one identical panel per pool: host, user, password, TLS, and
its own failover stepped independently of pool A's.

**Pool A's share is the Hashrate split slider and nothing else changes it.** It
defaults to 50, so a four-pool miner left at the defaults gives pool A half and
splits the rest three ways — about 16% each, not 25. The per-pool **Share**
weights divide only what the slider leaves over. Set the slider to 25 as well
for an even four-way split.

The dashboard shows every configured pool, each with its own connection badge
and share counts, and a split bar with a segment per pool. Pool A has a
connection badge for the first time, and `poolAConnected` joins
`pool<X>Connected` in `/api/system/info`.

## Smaller things you may notice

- **A task that fails to start now says so**, and the miner reports a hardware
  fault instead of reporting itself healthy while mining nothing.
- **The log viewer no longer appears to freeze** when lines arrive faster than
  it drew them.
- **Rejection reasons are shown.** The pool was sending them all along.
- **`POST /api/system/pool/drop`** ends a secondary pool's session on demand,
  so a reconnect can be tested without unplugging anything.
- **Internal RAM is reported** — `freeHeapInternal`, `minFreeHeapInternal`,
  `largestBlockInternal` and now the DMA equivalents. On this hardware the
  total free heap is almost all PSRAM and tells you nothing about the pool that
  actually runs out.
- The pool settings page no longer describes two pools, a split it does not
  have, or a failover belonging to pool B on pool C's panel.

## Still open

**On a BC04, a TLS read fails on the first HTTPS request after boot.** The
certificate validates, then `esp-aes: Failed to allocate memory` kills the read
and the request succeeds on a later endpoint in the same pass. It is not new in
this release and it is not fixed here — but it is now diagnosed. The AES path
needs a contiguous 1,600-byte DMA-capable buffer and the pool is fragmented: at
the moment of failure there were about 6 KB free in a largest block of 640.
Raising `SPIRAM_MALLOC_RESERVE_INTERNAL` to the IDF default was tried and did
not help, because the problem is fragmentation and not size.

**A BC01 does not show this**, which is the clearest evidence of what is really
going on. Checked on both boards: the BC01's first HTTPS request succeeds with
no failure line at all, because it has kilobytes of DMA-capable memory where a
BC04 has hundreds of bytes.

**A BC04's DMA-capable memory reaches tens of bytes during start-up.** Measured
across ten reboots, the floor is never more than about 1.6 KB and has been seen
at 19 bytes; four hours into the soak it reads 359. A BC01 on the same build
and the same elapsed time reads 7,099. There is no headroom left to trim for on
a BC04, and trimming stacks does not address it — 4,608 bytes of task stack
were returned during this release and the floor did not move.

**A down pool's slices are not shared out among the pools still up.** Work a
secondary cannot take falls through to pool A, not to the other secondaries.
Nothing is lost; the hashrate just lands somewhere other than where the weights
asked.

**Pool A rejects are still unexplained.** Small counts appear against the local
pool on both miners — four and three respectively, four hours into the soak,
against roughly four thousand accepted — and have never been traced. No
secondary pool has rejected a share on either board.

**The dashboard has no cards for pools C and D**, and its A/B split bar ignores
them — read `pool<X>JobsSelected` and `pool<X>JobsServed` from the API instead.

All of these, with what is known and what is not, are in
`docs/KNOWN-ISSUES.md`.

## Testing

SOAK_PLACEHOLDER

**What was verified on hardware rather than reasoned about.** The pool A fix
was measured through a Stratum relay standing in for the pool, with it
unreachable for 75 seconds: `poolAJobsServed` read the same at the moment the
session closed and at the moment the replacement session sent its first notify,
so nothing was built in between, and 41 submits came back with none rejected.
The secondary-pool fixes were measured the same way over eight forced
reconnects across all three secondary pools — nothing built before a fresh
extranonce existed, a distinct extranonce every time, nothing rejected, and the
other pools undisturbed.

The four-pool split was measured on a BC04 with the slider at 25 and each Share
at 25: within 1σ of 25% each over 2,754 shares with none rejected across two
hours.

Stack sizes were measured on both boards rather than guessed — the high-water
mark of every task read three times per board, and the sizes set from the worst
reading seen.

**What was not.** The browser flasher's Update mode has still never been run on
hardware in either direction; see `docs/KNOWN-ISSUES.md`. The upload retry has
still not been seen to fire on a real dropped upload.

## Files

`-full.bin` is the whole flash and is what the web flasher writes over USB.
`-ota.bin` and `-www-ota.bin` are for updating in place. `SHA256SUMS` covers
every file here; check it before you flash something you downloaded.
